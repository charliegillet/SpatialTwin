# Let your own footsteps grade Argon's twin

The biggest single improvement to WalkTwin is to stop letting Argon grade its own loop. **Run local structure-from-motion on the same walkthrough (pycolmap, BSD, Apple Silicon wheels) to recover the walker's camera path. Then score each render-critique iteration by geometry Argon never controls.** The main signal is every place the walker passed through a modelled wall where no door exists. That is a provable, timestamped error, so "illegal wall crossings 3 → 0" becomes a measured loop score that anyone can recompute. This lifts the 50% live-demo criterion from a "model-reported" count to a real verification loop. It also separates WalkTwin from the two render-compare-retry entries at GPT-6 Astra SF that did not place (Recurse.run and Farallon), and it gives the demo its best visual: a "ghost walker" that flashes red where it walks through a wall. The next three gains are cheap. First, recast the user as the crowd manager doing the pre-opening walk, with a binary decision tonight. Second, cut the nine-stage pipeline to a three-beat, three-minute demo. Third, chain every critique turn onto the extraction interaction, so Argon judges renders against the whole walkthrough held in context instead of eight stills. For the $5,000 Managed Agents prize, the agent should run broad what-if sweeps in its own sandbox, and the local checker should verify a shortlist. Today the plan has it call two client functions on a 20,000-token budget, which reads as a thin wrapper. The plan also contradicts Google's documentation in several places that change the build: the agent accepts images, not text only; the 20K budget and the 32K output cap are too low; Google recommends static rather than agentic video for clips under five minutes; and the solver applies SFPE specific flow to clear width rather than effective width. None of the judging weights, the Problem Statement Two text or the banned-project list could be checked against a public source, so they rest on the participant brief alone.

Read the table in three tiers. **Ranks 1, 2, 6 and 8 cost about four hours together.** They fix the story, the honesty of the numbers and the pitch, and none of them adds new technology risk. **Ranks 3 and 4 are the one big build worth committing a lane to.** Rank 3 alone has the highest weighted effect in both rounds, and rank 4 is its visible face. **Rank 7 is the prize play**, and ranks 9–12 are only for a team that is ahead by mid-afternoon.

The geometry loop wins because it is the only change that improves all four criteria at once. The plan currently labels the live loop score "model-reported" and keeps the measured score for an offline benchmark ([Plan](/Users/charlie/hackathons/SpatialTwin/docs/superpowers/plans/2026-10-25-walkthrough-twin.md)). A judge whose own work is factuality and evaluation design, such as Subhasmita Sahoo ([subhasmitasahoo.com](https://subhasmitasahoo.com/)), will ask whether the loop is grading itself. The answer becomes concrete. **Freeze one similarity transform from the SfM frame to the graph frame after iteration 0**: gravity from the floor plane, scale from the tape-measured anchor door, and yaw and translation from a one-time fit. Store its hash with the run, so score gains cannot come from re-alignment. Then compute three integers and fractions per iteration from SfM points at wall height. These are the share of points within 0.15 m of a modelled wall, the share falling outside every zone rectangle, and the number of camera-path crossings of modelled walls more than 0.5 m from a modelled door. The walker cannot pass through a wall, so every such crossing is a timestamped error. Add a 2D edge-chamfer score on frames the critique never saw. It projects the twin's floor-wall lines and door jambs through the SfM poses onto Canny or LSD edges, which acts like a train/test split. The Builder Guide's own advice matches this: give Argon a pass/fail signal, record a baseline and iterate until every check passes with zero regressions ([Builder Guide](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kN0n6hPNNTIHMVqf5-hDACrZuIg4KFrWiYpla4WQW8w)). The pieces are permissive and Mac-ready. **pycolmap 4.2.1 ships macOS arm64 wheels under BSD-3-Clause** ([PyPI pycolmap](https://pypi.org/project/pycolmap/)), and COLMAP 4.x includes the GLOMAP global mapper ([COLMAP 4.0 coverage](https://radiancefields.com/colmap-4.0-introduces-major-performance-and-infrastructure-updates)). OpenCV's LSD detector is back in current 4.x releases ([OpenCV LSD docs](https://docs.opencv.org/4.x/db/d73/classcv_1_1LineSegmentDetector.html)). Do not use SSIM or LPIPS between untextured renders and photos as the score. They mostly measure the appearance gap and furniture, not geometry.

Several attractive options score near zero and should be cut. **Long decode adds nothing to the live path.** Google says the first 64K tokens take about ten minutes when streaming, and it advises against long decode for extraction and for agents that make many short tool calls ([Long decode](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/long-decode)). A three.js twin fits well under the 65,536-token synchronous cap ([Argon model page](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/models/gemini-4-argon)). Maps grounding is a text search with mandatory source display, and its support table omits Argon ([Maps grounding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/maps-grounding)). Computer use gives imprecise camera poses where a `render_view` function gives exact ones ([Computer use](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/computer-use)). Learned layout models are either non-commercial or need CUDA: SpatialLM's point-cloud encoders are CC-BY-NC-4.0 and tested on CUDA 12.4 ([SpatialLM](https://github.com/manycore-research/SpatialLM)), and SceneScript is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([SceneScript LICENSE](https://github.com/facebookresearch/scenescript)). Phone-browser pose capture through WebXR works only on Android Chrome over HTTPS, because iPhone Safari has no WebXR AR ([W3C raw camera access](https://immersive-web.github.io/raw-camera-access/); [WWDC24](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2024/10066/)).

## Revised concept: Argon proposes, your footsteps dispose, the crowd manager decides

The revised user is **the venue ops lead or designated crowd manager doing the walk before doors open at a venue whose layout changes for every event**. The regulatory record gives this person a sharp, recurring gap. San Francisco requires a Place of Assembly permit for any event space with a capacity of 50 or more ([SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/fil/apply-for-a-fire-permit-special-event)). SFFD Administrative Bulletin 2.14 requires a dimensioned floor plan showing the means of egress, seating and occupant load, filed at least five business days ahead and followed by an inspection. The bulletin says nothing about re-checking when the layout changes on the day ([SFFD AB 2.14](https://sf-fire.org/214-submittal-guidelines-and-requirements-fire-permit-applications)). Model codes require at least one trained crowd manager in assembly occupancies, plus one per 250 occupants above 250 ([Frederick County MD](https://www.frederickcountymd.gov/index.asp?NID=2232)). Their duties include keeping every means of egress clear and accessible ([Ocean City MD](https://oceancitymd.gov/oc/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/3220)). The commonest night-time inspection finding is an obstruction in front of an exit ([Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/2013/02/02/brazil-fire-prompts-bend-inspections)). That is binary and visible on video, which suits video extraction far better than millimetre widths do. Existing tools leave the moment empty. Pathfinder costs **$2,350 a year node-locked** (the online store lists $2,500) and expects a modelled building ([Thunderhead licensing](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/pathfinder/licensing)). No egress-capacity calculation was found in Social Tables or Prismm ([Prismm](https://prismm.com/solutions/event-design-software/dynamic-3d-event-diagramming)). RoomPlan needs LiDAR and records geometry but not whether a door is chained ([Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/roomplan)).

The decision WalkTwin supports tonight is "open doors, or first unlock, clear or staff exit X." The output should read like the crowd manager's own checklist. It lists every exit found, with its state and a video timestamp, and says explicitly what was not filmed. It gives the checked count that reaches a modelled exit under a hazard on the main exit, the single allowed change that most improves that count, and a drafted line to security such as "D3 must stay unlocked and clear; checked 18:42; video t=01:12". It then turns the cannot-measure list into a hand-check to-do list: thresholds, hardware, opening force, signage. The Station nightclub fire is the cleanest story to tell. NIST found that about two-thirds of occupants tried the main entrance, and a crush there almost entirely blocked flow, even though other exits existed ([NIST](https://www.nist.gov/disaster-failure-studies/station-nightclub-fire-2003)). That is the per-door flow mechanism the solver represents. The pitch may say that the documented failure is an exit that existed on the plan but was unusable on the night. It must never say WalkTwin would have saved lives, because density and command failures sit on the cannot-measure list.

**Pitch it as a space audit with a measured verdict, not as a "3D twin" project.** The earlier winner analysis puts 3D/twin as a primary archetype at a 7% first-place rate (1 of 15) and space or plan compliance audits at 50% (n = 6). The samples are small, but the direction is consistent ([local winner analysis](/Users/charlie/hackathons/SpatialTwin/docs/research/winner-feature-analysis-2026-10-24.md)). The 3D entries that placed in recent galleries all fed a measured or actionable answer. SplatForge's twin led to a physics-measured success rate ([AIEWF #18](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/aiewf-hackathon-2026/hackathon/gallery/18)), Nirman.AI traced every solid to a figured dimension and said so when one was missing ([Bangalore #9](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/google-deepmind-bangalore-hackathon/hackathon/gallery/9)), and Wayfinder turned a scan into routes ([Astra London #28](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/openai-gpt-6-astra-london/hackathon/gallery/28)). Recurse.run, which renders, compares with a reference and retries, did not place ([Astra SF #47](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/openai-gpt-6-astra-sf/hackathon/gallery/47)). The one-line framing: **the twin is how Argon checks its own reading, your footsteps are how we check Argon, and the solver is how we measure.**

The same framing keeps WalkTwin clear of the banned types. A tool that reads a video and lists doors is close to an "image analyzer". The defence is that the deliverable is a decision with a checked consequence and a human approval, produced by a loop, not a caption. The requirements table is the most dashboard-like screen, so it belongs in Q&A while the twin and the video stay the hero. Domestic venues are fine as a benchmark venue, which answers the likely "does it work in a home?" question from a judge who builds Gemini for Home devices ([LinkedIn snippet](https://www.linkedin.com/in/shudhatma-jain-4381012a)). Aging-in-place or home-health framings drift toward medical judgment and should be avoided.

### The three-beat demo

Joana Carrasqueira, the only judge with public judging advice, says judges focus primarily on the demo. Their advice is to start with the why, and to deliver on the hook you set up ([X post](https://x.com/jocarrasqueira/status/1987577252725543314)). The current runbook packs nine stages into 180 seconds. The revised one has three beats, each ending on one witnessed state change.

| Beat | Time | What the judge sees | What to say | Criterion |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Your walk becomes the twin | 0:00–0:55 | Hook in 12 words. The judge films about 40 s of this room. Their clip goes to local SfM, and their own path appears as a line while Argon's extraction runs. Then the saved, timestamped full-floor run opens, and clicking a door's evidence seeks the video to that door. | "Every event moves the tables. Nobody re-checks the exits. Film the room." | Problem, live real input |
| 2. Argon proposes, geometry disposes | 0:55–1:50 | The ghost walker passes through a wall at 0:37 and flashes red. Argon's patch adds D4, cited at 0:37, and the twin re-renders. Illegal crossings drop 3 → 0 and held-out edge alignment improves. One patch is rejected, with its reason. | "Argon compared its twin with the whole video. Your footsteps checked Argon. Before the loop, N of M exits; after, M of M." (real benchmark numbers only) | Verification loop, originality |
| 3. Fire, fix, approve | 1:50–2:50 | The judge drops Fire on the main exit, and the hero number falls, marked checked. The agent's sandbox sweep and the client checker's verified shortlist appear side by side. The proposal is to open D3; the judge clicks its evidence and sees D3 chained in the footage, then approves. The number recovers, and the note appears headed "DRAFT — not sent". | "The agent investigated, our checker measured, you approved. Nothing leaves this laptop." | Usefulness, human control, Managed Agents |

The closing ten seconds go to the judge's own clip. Show its result, or state its measured stage time honestly ("this takes about N minutes; here is the run from 09:40"). Carrasqueira's advice treats an honest "here is how we would take it further" as survivable. A missed hook is not. Three moves cut the latency risk, which is the likeliest failure. **Send the judge's clip to local SfM before Argon finishes**, so the ghost walker appears early, though pycolmap's runtime on a few hundred 720p frames is an estimate, not a measurement. **Start the agent's sweep in the background as soon as the graph is accepted**, one investigation per plausible single-door hazard, each tagged with its own scenario hash. When the judge picks a door, only the sub-second client checker runs live, and the plan's stale-revision rule still holds because each investigation carries its own revision and hash. Finally, **keep the ADA rows, the report and the benchmark table one click away for Q&A** rather than in the three minutes.

Prepare one sentence per judge lens. For Sahoo, on invented doors: every element carries a video timestamp, a human accepts the graph, the footsteps catch missed doors, and precision and recall are reported against tape-measured keys. For Oskar Yao, who is on the AI Futures Fund team ([AI Futures Fund](https://labs.google/aifuturesfund)): the buyer is the venue operator, the trigger recurs at every layout change, and today's practice is a visual walk with no record. Do not quote invented market sizes.

## The managed agent should earn its $5,000 inside its own sandbox

Ivan Leo, who led Gemini Managed Agents, is listed as a speaker, not a judge ([event page](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/gemini-4-argon-sf)). The prize criteria are unpublished. What Google itself promotes is clear, though. The launch posts stress the hosted sandbox and harness, and the July expansion added environment hooks that block, lint or audit tool calls, `max_total_tokens` budgets that end `incomplete` and resume through `previous_interaction_id`, scheduled triggers and an Environments API ([Google blog, 28 Jul 2026](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/developers-tools/expanding-managed-agents-gemini-api-3-6-flash-hooks/)). The documented agent gets a **4-CPU, 16 GB Ubuntu sandbox** with code execution, mounted Git, GCS or inline sources, network allowlists or a fully disabled network, `AGENTS.md`, skills and saved agents ([Agent environment](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/agent-environment); [Custom agents](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/custom-agents)). The plan uses none of it: text-only input, two client functions and a 20,000-token budget.

The upgrade keeps the plan's authority rule intact and gives the agent real work. Mount the solver, graph checks and scenario JSON into the sandbox from the public repo, or inline when under 2 MB. Let Argon use `code_execution` there for broad sweeps: door combinations, alternative hazard placements, ±20% sensitivity on the declared flow and walk-speed parameters, and a bottleneck ranking. It then sends a shortlist to the client function `evaluate_interventions`, whose solver and independent checker remain the only source of numbers on screen. **Show the sandbox figure and the checker figure side by side and flag any disagreement.** A visible disagreement is a verification moment, not an embarrassment. The pitch line becomes "Argon ran N scenarios in its own sandbox; our checker verified the ones it recommends," with N taken from the actual ledger.

The documentation fixes several mechanics that the plan does not yet handle. **Passing `tools` replaces the default set**, so `code_execution` must be listed explicitly, and `google_search` and `url_context` should be left out so the agent does not browse off-task ([Antigravity agent](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/antigravity-agent#supported-tools)). Filesystem tools show up as `function_call` steps but run automatically, so the client must execute only allowlisted names ([Function calling](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/antigravity-agent#function-calling)). The agent supports no structured output, so a client function `submit_recommendation(intervention_id, rationale, caveats[])` returns the final answer schema-shaped instead of as prose to parse ([Limitations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/antigravity-agent#limitations)).

The sandbox comes with numpy and pandas but **not networkx or pydantic** ([Pre-installed software](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/agent-environment#pre-installed-software)). The clean path is to set up the environment once with network access, then reuse or fork it and run investigations with `network: "disabled"`: a reusable investigator with no internet access. Saved agents are the stretch version, because the custom-agents limitations list only Flash models while the Antigravity page lists Argon ([Custom agents limitations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/custom-agents#limitations); [Model selection](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/antigravity-agent#model-selection)). `AGENTS.md` carries the copy rules and the rule that numbers come only from `evaluate_interventions`. Skills carry the sweep method and static ADA section text, so citations never depend on web search.

One `pre_tool_execution` hook that denies writes to the accepted graph produces a denial worth showing. Present hooks honestly. They fail open on a crash or timeout, they do not fire for custom function tools, and the agent can rewrite them unless they are mounted read-only ([Hooks](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/agent-hooks)), so they are audit and policy guidance while the authority layer stays local. The agent can also take a top-down render with the hazard and per-door flows as an inline base64 image, which the docs allow. The investigation panel should stream `code_execution` and `function_call` steps as evidence, never a fake timer.

One walkthrough is only about 15K–45K tokens (about 100 tokens per second at default resolution, 300 at high), so "1M context" is hollow for a single clip. It becomes real when one stateful interaction holds the video, every iteration's renders and discrepancies, and the graph ([Video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding#multi-turn-video-conversations)).

## Risks, licenses and claims nobody has verified yet

**Latency is the top risk.** Agentic extraction, a critique loop and an Argon managed agent all have to show progress inside three minutes, and none has been timed. Google says agentic navigation can raise time to first token on short clips, and long agentic runs should use background mode ([Video understanding](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/video-understanding)). The saved live run, labelled with its timestamp, is the right fallback. The local SfM fast path and the background agent pre-computation shrink the live critical path to what is local. **The second risk is a flat benchmark.** Google's own guidance favours static mode for clips this short, so "agentic beats static" could fail on 90–150 s walkthroughs. Winners like AGI Factory and SPARKLETEAM placed with honest measured deltas ([Crusoe gallery](https://cerebralvalley.ai/e/crusoe-recursive-agents-hackathon/hackathon/gallery)). The fallback is a permissive feed-forward model on MPS whose speed is unmeasured. **Metric models alone are too coarse for ADA thresholds.** MapAnything's paper reports about 16% average relative scale error ([arXiv 2509.13414](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.13414v1)), against a 32-inch threshold that a few centimetres decide. ## Conclusion

The research changes what WalkTwin's loop is for. The plan treats render-versus-video critique as the Problem Statement Two box to tick and admits its score is self-reported. The evidence says critique loops alone do not place, and that 3D work wins when it feeds a measured answer. The walkthrough already contains an answer key the model cannot fake: the path of the person holding the phone. Using it turns "render, watch, critique, fix" from an aesthetic loop into an epistemic one. Argon proposes structure, physical motion disposes, and only then does the solver measure and the crowd manager decide. This is also the most defensible novelty claim, because no reviewed winner chained video evidence, a structural model, a measured consequence and a human decision, with a deterministic check at each link.

The practical implication is ordering. Spend the first hours on what is nearly free: the user, the three beats, the honest baselines and the solver corrections. Commit one lane to SfM and the geometry score. Treat the sandbox agent as a separate bet on a separate $5,000. A team that ships only the first tier still has a sharper project than the current plan. A team that ships the geometry loop has the one live number a factuality-minded judge cannot wave away.
