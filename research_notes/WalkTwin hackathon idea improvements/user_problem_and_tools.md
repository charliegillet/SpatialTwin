# WalkTwin: user, problem space and existing tools

Research date: 25 October 2026. Scope: US / San Francisco where jurisdiction matters. Nothing here should be read as approving a real venue for occupancy. Where the only sources were secondary (consultant blogs, checklists, press), that is stated.

## 1. Temporary event layouts: what permits and checks apply (SF and US), and who re-checks

### Takeaway
In San Francisco, any event space with a capacity of 50 or more needs a Place of Assembly permit. A temporary permit needs a *dimensioned floor plan showing the means of egress, the use of each area, the seating arrangement and the proposed occupant load*, filed at least five business days ahead, followed by an SFFD inspection. The approved plan applies to one specific use. The bulletin says nothing about re-checking when the layout changes on the day. Model codes put that job on trained crowd managers, who must keep egress clear. The gap: the plan is checked days before the event, and nobody measures the layout that actually gets built.

### Cited Findings
- SF: if a venue's maximum capacity is 50 people or more, it needs a temporary or permanent Place of Assembly permit from the Fire Department — [SF.gov, fire permit for special event](https://www.sf.gov/fil/apply-for-a-fire-permit-special-event)
- SFFD Administrative Bulletin 2.14 requirements for a temporary/special Place of Assembly: a "dimensioned floor plan of the space requested showing details of the means of egress facilities, use of each area, seating arrangement, and proposed occupant load"; written permission from the legal owner; a Supplemental Application for Special Events; general liability insurance of $1M per occurrence with the City named as additional insured — [SFFD AB 2.14](https://sf-fire.org/214-submittal-guidelines-and-requirements-fire-permit-applications)
- AB 2.14 timing and inspection: submit at least five business days before the start (shorter timelines may incur overtime review fees); "an SFFD inspection is required before any fire permit is approved"; accepting an application is not approval to operate; the fire code official may require a fire watch at owner cost (overtime rates, 4-hour minimum); breaking permit conditions voids the permit; changes to holder, activity type, address, date or duration need a new application. The bulletin does not address aisle widths, locked doors or layout changes after submission — [SFFD AB 2.14](https://sf-fire.org/214-submittal-guidelines-and-requirements-fire-permit-applications)
- A permanent Place of Assembly submits the approved floor plan, no larger than 11×17 in, showing the application number, occupancy classification and occupant load — [SFFD AB 2.14](https://sf-fire.org/214-submittal-guidelines-and-requirements-fire-permit-applications)
- Fee: sf-fire.org lists $446 for Place of Assembly, effective 9/1/2025. A SF.gov page lists $436, so the two sources differ slightly — [SFFD Permits](https://sf-fire.org/services/permits); [SF.gov](https://www.sf.gov/fil/apply-for-a-fire-permit-special-event)
- Tents and membrane structures with an occupant load of 50 or more need a detailed site map and floor plan covering occupant load justification, egress width and location, seating layout, emergency lighting, exit signs and extinguishers. The page cites the 2022 San Francisco Fire Code — [SFFD AB 2.13](https://sf-fire.org/213-submittal-requirements-temporary-tents-and-membrane-structures)
- Indoor events in SF may also need an Entertainment Commission permit — [SF.gov entertainment permit](https://www.sf.gov/get-entertainment-permit-your-indoor-event)
- Other US jurisdictions follow the same pattern. Seattle requires a temporary assembly permit when a venue holding an annual assembly permit changes its exits, occupant load, use or activity, and approves the attached floor plan "only for a specific use", subject to field inspection — [Seattle Fire, Special Events](https://seattle.gov/fire/business-services/special-events); [Seattle permit conditions](https://www.seattle.gov/documents/departments/fire/business/permitcond_2503-2505.pdf). San Diego requires plans that show all exits and entrances, drawn to scale or dimensioned — [San Diego Fire special events](https://www.sandiego.gov/sites/default/files/legacy/fire/pdf/specialevents.pdf). Dallas requires fully dimensioned drawings and exit hardware details at least 45 days ahead — [Dallas temporary assembly packet](https://dallasculture.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/04/Temporary-Assembly-Permit-Info-Packet-2019.pdf)
- Crowd managers: NFPA 1 (2021), cross-referencing NFPA 101 §12.7.6.1/13.7.6.1, requires at least one trained crowd manager in assembly occupancies, plus 1 per 250 occupants above 250. The AHJ may reduce this for sprinklered buildings. Local thresholds vary (Maryland: occupant load over 50; Georgia: 100 or more) — [Frederick County MD Fire Marshal](https://www.frederickcountymd.gov/index.asp?NID=2232); [Maryland State Fire Marshal](https://stg-mdsp.maryland.gov/firemarshal/Pages/CrowdManagerTraining.aspx)
- Crowd manager duties include "ensuring all means of egress are clear and accessible, as well as marked and illuminated" — [Ocean City MD](https://oceancitymd.gov/oc/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/3220). Under NFPA 101 §7.5.2 egress impediments such as curtains, poster boards and stanchions need an AHJ-approved deployment plan or must be removed — [UTSA AHJ memo](https://www.utsa.edu/rpm/services/safety-risk-management/docs/fire-life/event-mgmt-crowd-control-for-assemblies-ahj-memo.pdf)
- In field inspections, the most frequent night-time finding is "something in front of the exit" (Bend, OR deputy fire marshal). Burlington, VT names overcrowding as the most common violation and also checks nightly for locked or obstructed exits. DC nightclub violations included blocked exits and poorly lit exit signs — [Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/2013/02/02/brazil-fire-prompts-bend-inspections); [Seven Days VT](https://www.sevendaysvt.com/music/hot-spots-2127743/); [GW Hatchet](https://www.gwhatchet.com/2003/03/10/d-c-enforces-nightclub-codes/)
- In 2026, a Czech nationwide inspection campaign found 2,381 deficiencies at 729 establishments between January and August. Many involved blocked or poorly marked escape routes — [Expats.cz](https://www.expats.cz/czech-news/article/prague-nightclub-checks-raise-alarms-over-hundreds-of-safety-faults)

### Inferences
- SF's real governing text is the San Francisco Fire Code, based on the California Fire Code, which derives from the IFC (AB 2.13 cites the 2022 SF Fire Code). NFPA 101 numbers are useful *general principles*. WalkTwin should not present them as "the SF rule".
- The submitted plan is a promise made days earlier, and the built layout can drift from it: stage moved, extra table block, side door chained for security. AB 2.14 has no on-the-day re-check step. NFPA's crowd-manager duty is the closest existing "re-check", and it is a visual walk with no measurement. WalkTwin fits that moment: a phone walkthrough at the time the crowd manager already walks the floor.
- The most common field finding (an obstruction in front of an exit, or a locked exit) is exactly what WalkTwin's hazard and intervention model represents. A blocked or locked door is binary and visible on video, so it suits video extraction better than precise widths do.
- A second moment where WalkTwin helps is *permit preparation*: a pop-up producer in a non-assembly space (warehouse, office, gallery) must submit a dimensioned egress floor plan in five business days and often has no CAD. A video-derived draft plan with explicit "estimated" and "cannot measure" labels could feed a professional's submission. WalkTwin must never present it as the permit plan.

### Gaps
- I found no data on how often layouts change between permit submission and doors-open, or how often the built layout differs from the approved plan.
- I didn't retrieve SF-specific aisle-width or locked-door rules (CFC Ch. 10 equivalents). AB 2.14 says to call the Operational Permit Section (628) 652-3260.
- I didn't verify whether SFFD inspects every temporary PoA before each event or only on the first occasion; AB 2.14 says only that an inspection is required before approval.

## 2. Crowd incidents and lessons where exits, locks or layout changes mattered

### Takeaway
In every incident below, the reported failure involves exits that existed on paper but were unusable, undersized, unknown to occupants, or narrowed by a change nobody re-checked. The record supports WalkTwin's core premise (the as-built layout matters, not the plan). It does not support claiming WalkTwin would have prevented any of them, because several were dominated by crowd density, fire spread or command failures that WalkTwin explicitly cannot measure.

### Cited Findings
- **The Station nightclub, West Warwick RI, 2003 (100 deaths):** NIST found that before 90 s a crowd crush at the main entrance "almost entirely disrupted" flow through the front exit; about two-thirds of occupants tried to leave through the main entrance and many failed, although other exits existed beside the platform, at the side of the main bar and in the kitchen. NIST recommended that model codes require main exits sized for a larger share of the occupant load — [NIST, Station Nightclub Fire](https://www.nist.gov/disaster-failure-studies/station-nightclub-fire-2003). Commentary says the combined exit capacity was about 420, slightly below the number of occupants, and that there is no indication patrons were told about other exits — [Fire Engineering](https://www.fireengineering.com/firefighting/station-nightclub-fire-revisiting-lessons-full/)
- A current plan-review checklist sizes the NFPA 101 assembly main exit for 1/2 the occupant load in general and 2/3 for dance halls, discotheques, nightclubs and festival seating (NFPA 101 §12.2.3.6) — [Decatur GA assembly plan review](https://www.decaturga.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/fire/page/8146/plan_reveiw_assembly_spaces.pdf). (Secondary; consistent with the post-Station change.)
- **Ghost Ship, Oakland CA, December 2016 (36 deaths):** officials said the warehouse had only two exits and the floors were linked by an "ad hoc staircase made of wooden pallets"; the fire chief said it had no sprinklers or alarms; city records reviewed by the LA Times indicated the building did not meet construction codes — [The Root/AP](https://www.theroot.com/33-confirmed-dead-in-oakland-calif-warehouse-fire); [Pollstar](https://news.pollstar.com/?p=260803). In 2019 Max Harris was acquitted and the jury hung on Derick Almena. Prosecutors argued he resisted inspections; the defense argued arson and that city officials had inspected and found no violations — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/news/us-news/ghost-ship-warehouse-fire-trial-verdict-one-man-acquitted-manslaughter-n1038126); [KTVU](https://ktvu.com/news/ghost-ship-trial-max-harris-acquitted-jury-hung-on-derick-almena)
- **Astroworld, Houston, November 2021 (crowd surge, at least 8 deaths at the time of early reports):** the Houston fire chief said that under fire codes the site could hold 200,000 but attendance was capped at 50,000, and that "crowd control at the point of the stage" was the issue; attendees described barricades near the stage trapping people — [Boston Globe/AP](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2021/11/06/nation/8-dead-many-injured-astroworld-fest-texas-officials-say/). The Texas Task Force on Concert Safety (April 2022) recommended a centralized on-site command group with authority to halt an event and a universal statewide permit template; officials said no permits were obtained for the festival — [Texas Governor, Task Force report](https://gov.texas.gov/music/page/task-force-on-concert-safety-report); [10News/Scripps](https://www.10news.com/news/national/safety-task-force-issues-recommendations-months-after-deadly-astroworld-concert). Plaintiffs' experts argued planners misunderstood how many people they could legally fit — [Houston Landing](https://houstonlanding.org/astroworld-planners-foresaw-crowding-before-deadly-festival-no-way-we-are-going-to-fit-50k)
- **Itaewon, Seoul, October 2022 (158–159 deaths; figures vary):** investigators said the crush began where the sloped alley was narrower than 3.2 m, and reports mention an iron temporary wall that narrowed the street further; density reached about 8 p/m² at 22:15 and 9–11 p/m² at 22:25 (Korea Times peak 10.74 p/m²); police called it a "man-made" disaster from failures in prevention and response — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/nation/2023/09/113_343539.html); [SCMP](https://amp.scmp.com/news/asia/east-asia/article/3206680/botched-response-blamed-deadly-seoul-halloween-crush-23-officials-face-charges); [Wikipedia summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seoul_Halloween_crowd_crush)
- **Pulse nightclub, Kočani, North Macedonia, March 2025 (59 deaths):** the state prosecutor said the club had "only one single improvised metal door at the back of the building, which was locked and without a handle on the inside", two extinguishers and no alarm; a preliminary inspection found missing emergency exits and poor emergency-vehicle access; the club lacked a proper licence; the government ordered nationwide nightclub inspections — [MarketScreener/Reuters](https://in.marketscreener.com/news/latest/North-Macedonia-nightclub-where-blaze-killed-59-lacked-safety-measures-says-prosecutor-49350542/); [NY1](https://ny1.com/nyc/all-boroughs/international/2025/03/17/north-macedonia-nightclub-fire-investigation)
- **Le Constellation, Crans-Montana, Switzerland, New Year 2026 (about 40–41 deaths; one headline says 47):** investigators reportedly found 34 victims were trapped after a cramped basement staircase collapsed; the stairwell had been "narrowed by two-thirds by the owner after renovations"; photos reportedly show a service door locked and an emergency exit blocked (the operators dispute this); the commune found "periodic checks were not carried out between 2020 and 2025"; manslaughter-by-negligence proceedings opened — [LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/world-news/majority-swiss-victims-staircase-collapse); [blue News](https://www.bluewin.ch/en/news/switzerland/new-pictures-show-the-destruction-in-the-bar-and-raise-questions-3092864.html); [The Local CH](https://www.thelocal.ch/20260120/whats-the-latest-on-the-swiss-investigation-into-deadly-bar-fire)

### Inferences
- The incidents fall into three patterns. WalkTwin addresses the first two: (a) exits that were locked, blocked or unknown (Kočani, Station side exits, Crans-Montana alleged); (b) layout changes that narrowed a route without re-checking (Crans-Montana stair, Itaewon temporary wall); and (c) density, surge and command failures (Astroworld, Itaewon), which WalkTwin's cannot-measure list already excludes ("crowd density effects").
- Station is the cleanest fit for the demo: the problem was not too few doors in total but an unused distribution across them. WalkTwin's solver result (per-door flow, limiting doors, and the effect of opening or signposting another door) represents the same mechanism. The pitch can cite NIST's "two-thirds tried the main entrance" without dramatizing.
- Pitch carefully. It is fair to say "the documented failure mode is an exit that was on the plan but not usable on the night". It is not fair to say WalkTwin "would have saved lives".

### Gaps
- I didn't retrieve the Houston Police Department's 2023 Astroworld report or the final Itaewon Special Investigation Headquarters report. Figures above come from press summaries.
- No final official cause determination for Ghost Ship surfaced in my searches. Kočani and Crans-Montana findings are preliminary (prosecutor and press statements up to about January 2026).
- I found no US incident from 2024–2026 with an official report centred on locked exits. The recent cases above are European.

## 3. Existing tools: cost, requirements and gaps

### Takeaway
There are three tool families, and none connects a phone capture of tonight's as-built layout to an egress result. Egress simulators (Pathfinder and peers) are engineer tools: they cost thousands per seat and expect a CAD/BIM model. Event diagramming tools (Social Tables/Cvent, Prismm/AllSeated) draw to-scale layouts but have no egress-capacity calculation that I could find. Scanners (Matterport, Polycam, RoomPlan) produce geometry without exits, door states or flow. WalkTwin's distinct value is the connection: video, then a venue graph with door states and evidence timestamps, then a checked flow result, all with stated uncertainty.

### Cited Findings
- **Pathfinder (Thunderhead):** licensing page: annual subscription $2,350 node-locked / $3,525 floating; perpetual $7,850 / $11,775 plus maintenance $1,570 / $2,355. The online store lists higher figures ($2,500 / $3,750 annual; $8,350 / $12,525 perpetual). Confirm with sales — [Thunderhead licensing](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/pathfinder/licensing); [Thunderhead store](https://store2.thunderheadeng.com/cart)
- Pathfinder's SFPE mode uses SFPE Handbook 5th ed. (2016) relations: default maximum room density 3.55 p/m²; the evacuation speed density is clamped to 1.9–3.0 p/m²; door flow uses a density of 1.88 p/m² (which maximizes flow) when calculated specific flow is off; stair k-values run 1.00–1.23 for 7.5/10 to 6.5/13 in riser/tread — [Pathfinder SFPE technical reference](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2026-1/pathfinder/appendices/technical-reference/sfpe)
- Pathfinder's default unimpeded walking speed is 1.19 m/s (3.9 ft/s); in an NFPA 130 comparison NFPA 130 uses 37.7 m/min and SFPE 43.4 m/min — [Pathfinder NFPA 130 comparison](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2026-1/pathfinder/examples/applications/comparing-nfpa-130/)
- A Pathfinder door-flow verification case applies a 15 cm boundary layer per SFPE guidelines and gets about 0.92 p/s for the test door in SFPE mode, against an IMO threshold of 1.33 p/s — [Pathfinder IMO_04](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2025-1/pathfinder/appendices/verification-validation/imo/imo-4)
- Model validity: a University of Maryland thesis found the SFPE Handbook velocity and specific-flow correlations (Nelson and Mowrer) acted as an upper limit on the stair data analyzed — [UMD DRUM thesis](https://drum.lib.umd.edu/handle/1903/10952). Bottleneck experiments found capacity depends on narrowing length and supply, not one density-flow relation — [arXiv 1508.06789](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1508.06789)
- **MassMotion (Oasys):** described as importing 2D and 3D assets to build a 3D simulation; no public price found — [B2B Stack listing](https://www.b2bstack.com.br/product/massmotion)
- **Event diagramming:** Prismm (formerly AllSeated) offers 2D/3D floor plans, drag-and-drop seating, about 10,000 objects and floor-plan reports tied to guest data — [Prismm](https://prismm.com/solutions/event-design-software/dynamic-3d-event-diagramming); [Perfect Venue integration](https://www.perfectvenue.com/integrations-and-partnerships/prismm-formerly-allseated). Social Tables (Cvent) offers to-scale layouts, seating and 6,700+ venue plans — [ExpoFP comparison (JP)](https://expofp.com/ja/best-event-diagramming-software). An AllSeated–Matterport partnership existed — [WeGetAround](https://wegetaroundnetwork.com/topic/16227/allseatedmatterport-delivers-30-increase-in-sales-for-allseated-partners). I found no documentation of an egress-capacity calculation in either.
- Occupancy limits for event spaces come from the fire department, not the layout software (Santa Barbara Carousel House: "the Santa Barbara Fire Department determined these numbers") — [Santa Barbara Parks](https://sbparksandrec.santabarbaraca.gov/sites/default/files/2022-06/Carousel%20House%20Floor%20Plan%20Tips.pdf)
- **Matterport:** pricing sources conflict. PricingSaaS lists Free / Starter $14 / Professional $69 / Business $355 per month; a tracker dated October 2026 reports Starter and Business removed, Professional repriced at 10 spaces for $90/month, and a $1,995 Pro3 bundle. Best quality needs a Pro2/Pro3 camera ($3,000–$5,000+) — [PricingSaaS](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/matterport); [PricingSaaS diff 2026W42](https://pricingsaas.com/companies/matterport/diffs/2026W42); [CORadvisors](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/04/blog-post.html)
- **Polycam:** Pro about $26.99/month; Business $34/month or $400/year; runs on iPhone/iPad LiDAR without dedicated hardware — [CORadvisors](https://www.coradvisors.net/2026/04/blog-post.html); [SkyeBrowse](https://www.skyebrowse.com/news/posts/polycam-vs-matterport)
- **Apple RoomPlan:** a Swift API that uses the camera and LiDAR to create a 3D floor plan with dimensions; one network detects walls and openings, another detects doors and windows — [Apple ML Research](https://machinelearning.apple.com/research/roomplan). An Apple forum reply says running without LiDAR would cause "a substantial loss in accuracy" — [Apple Developer Forums](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/776280). A third-party review of a RoomPlan app estimates about 1–3 cm in normal interiors and says results are not suitable for permit-grade drawings — [illustrarch](https://illustrarch.com/articles/design-softwares/110334-roomscan-pro-review.html). A Rhino developer reported LiDAR scans within 1–4 in — [McNeel forum](https://discourse.mcneel.com/t/introducing-room-scan/152378)

### Inferences
- **Gap 1: input cost.** Simulators need a modeled building and a trained user, and per-seat costs run to thousands. They fit design-stage studies by fire protection engineers, not a crowd manager's 6 pm walk.
- **Gap 2: semantics.** Scanners give surfaces and dimensions, and RoomPlan detects doors, but none records *door state* (locked/chained/blocked), *exit vs. interior door*, or *evidence timestamps*. Those are the egress-relevant facts and the ones incidents turn on.
- **Gap 3: event tools don't compute egress.** Event planners already draw to-scale layouts in Social Tables/Prismm. Their output is a picture for the client and the fire marshal, not a check.
- **What phone video uniquely enables:** works on any phone (no LiDAR needed, though RoomPlan widths would be more accurate); captures what the space looks like at that moment; leaves a reviewable time-stamped record ("D3 seen chained at 01:12"); takes minutes, not a site survey. The trade-off is weaker metric accuracy. The plan's anchor door and width bands are the right response. A RoomPlan/LiDAR width import is a natural product extension.
- WalkTwin should not compete with Pathfinder. A credible framing is "triage before the engineer and fire marshal, re-check after them". An export of the venue graph to a format a simulator or diagramming tool can import would support the "could grow into a product" criterion.

### Gaps
- No public pricing for MassMotion, buildingEXODUS or Legion; vendor quotes required. I didn't research FDS beyond noting it is NIST's free fire-dynamics code (not an egress model); not verified this session.
- No independent, controlled benchmark of RoomPlan door-width accuracy found.
- I couldn't confirm Social Tables/Prismm lack egress features beyond the absence of documentation.

## 4. Candidate users: decisions, fit with "measured + cannot measure", and ranking

### Takeaway
The sharpest user is the person who already has to walk the floor before doors open, the venue ops lead or trained crowd manager at a venue whose layout changes per event. Their decision is binary and urgent: open doors, or first fix a specific exit. A close second is the same organisation one step earlier, the pop-up producer preparing SFFD's dimensioned egress floor plan without CAD. Accessibility consultants are a strong fit for the ADA pass/fail/cannot-measure layer but not for the flow solver. Insurers, scouts and aging-in-place don't fit well enough.

### Cited Findings
- Crowd managers are required in assembly occupancies (1 per 250 above 250 under NFPA 1/101; lower local thresholds exist) and are responsible for egress being clear, marked and illuminated — [Frederick County MD](https://www.frederickcountymd.gov/index.asp?NID=2232); [Ocean City MD](https://oceancitymd.gov/oc/wp-json/wp/v2/pages/3220)
- SF temporary PoA permits need a dimensioned egress floor plan five business days ahead, plus inspection — [SFFD AB 2.14](https://sf-fire.org/214-submittal-guidelines-and-requirements-fire-permit-applications)
- Fire inspectors' most frequent night finding is an obstruction in front of an exit — [Bend Bulletin](https://bendbulletin.com/2013/02/02/brazil-fire-prompts-bend-inspections)
- California CASp (Certified Access Specialist) reports: state rulemaking estimated $1,500–$3,500 (midpoint $2,500) for facilities up to 10,000 ft²; a state guide says the small-business average is under $2,000; the inspection "provides legal benefits that may reduce a business' financial liability in a lawsuit filed in state court" — [CPCFA rulemaking](https://www.treasurer.ca.gov/CPCFA/calcap/ada/notice-of-proposed-rulemaking-3.22.18.pdf); [CA DGS Five Steps](https://www.dgs.ca.gov/-/media/Divisions/CCDA/CCDA-Miscellaneous-Organized/5-Steps-for-Business-Accessibility---1000-word.docx)
- ADA door items that need tools or physical access (not visible on video): thresholds ½ in max (¾ in existing, beveled), hardware operable one-handed with ≤5 lbf at 34–48 in, opening force 5 lbf for interior doors measured to 70°. Clear width is measured "from the stop to the face of doors... open 90°" — [US Access Board guide ch. 4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-entrances-doors-and-gates/)

### Inferences (ranked framings)

**1. Venue ops lead / trained crowd manager, pre-opening egress walk (recommended primary).**
- *Who:* the person at a multi-use venue (event space, warehouse venue, hotel ballroom, co-working event floor like SHACK15) who walks the floor before doors open, often the designated crowd manager.
- *Decision tonight:* "Can we open doors with this layout, or must we first unlock, staff or clear exit X?" Plus "Which door do we tell security must stay unlocked?"
- *Today:* a visual walk, maybe a printed permit plan, no measurement, no record.
- *What goes wrong:* a door chained for security, a stage or bar block narrowing a route, a staging area in front of a side exit. These are the commonest field findings and the Kočani/Crans-Montana pattern.
- *What WalkTwin must output:* (a) every exit found, with door state and video timestamp evidence; (b) any exit seen in the permit plan but not found or not usable ("not filmed / unreachable" must be explicit); (c) under a hazard on the main exit, the checked count reaching a modeled exit within the horizon and the limiting door; (d) the one change that most improves it; (e) a one-paragraph drafted note to security/staff ("D3 must stay unlocked and clear; checked at 18:42; video t=01:12"); (f) the cannot-measure list.
- *Why "measured + cannot measure" helps:* the crowd manager's job is a checklist, and an honest "cannot measure door hardware/signage, go check by hand" turns into a to-do list.

**2. Pop-up / temporary event producer preparing a permit submission (strong secondary; same buyer, earlier moment).**
- *Decision:* "Is this space plausibly workable for N people with this layout before I pay for a drafter or engineer and file with SFFD five business days out?"
- *Output:* a draft dimensioned plan with widths as bands, exits and egress routes, labelled "draft for a professional, not a permit drawing". Show the declared occupant-load factors next to the floor area so the producer sees roughly what capacity the space implies.
- *Risk:* never imply the plan is permit-ready or that the capacity is approved.

**3. Accessibility consultant / CASp, pre-survey triage.**
- *Decision:* "Which doors and routes need tape and gauge on my site visit, and how long will the visit take?" (Reports cost $1,500–$3,500.)
- *Fit:* excellent for ADA §404.2.3 / §403.5.1 / §304.3 width rows with pass/fail/cannot-measure, and the Access Board guide gives the cannot-measure items directly (thresholds, hardware, force). The egress flow solver adds little for them, so this weakens the hackathon story if used as the primary user.

**4. School or office emergency planner.**
- *Decision:* evacuation route and assembly-point plans, drill debriefs, what happens if stair B is unavailable.
- *Fit:* moderate. Layouts change less often than at events, so the "re-check tonight" urgency is lower, but the hazard-plus-intervention loop maps well onto "what if this exit is blocked" drill planning. No sources gathered on this workflow (gap).

**5. Fire inspector during a field inspection.**
- *Decision:* whether the built layout matches the approved plan and the permit conditions.
- *Fit:* good as an evidence record (time-stamped video of each exit), but WalkTwin must not enter the approval decision. A government buyer also slows the "could grow into a product" story. Better pitched as "the venue sends the inspector its WalkTwin record" than as the inspector's tool.

**Not recommended:**
- *Insurers / risk engineers:* no evidence gathered of an event-egress workflow they would act on (gap).
- *Film location scouts:* the decision ("can we shoot here") rarely turns on egress flow.
- *Aging-in-place home modification:* ADA widths would apply only loosely to private homes, and the framing drifts toward health or medical judgments, which the hackathon bans.

### Gaps
- No interviews or surveys of crowd managers or venue ops leads; I found no published data on how long their pre-opening walk takes or what they record.
- No sources on how insurers underwrite event egress risk, or whether they would accept a video record.
- No sources on school/office emergency-planner tooling.

## 5. Standard numbers a solver could cite, and the public-domain status of code texts

### Takeaway
Two kinds of numbers are defensible. ADA 2010 width thresholds come from the Access Board's own guide. NFPA 101/IBC capacity factors and occupant-load factors come from secondary sources, because the code texts weren't directly accessible. The plan's `specific_flow = 1.3 p/m/s` is consistent with SFPE practice but is applied to *effective* width (clear width minus boundary layers) in SFPE. Applying it to clear width overstates flow. Label all of these "declared" and cite them as general engineering values, not SF code.

### Cited Findings
- **ADA 2010 (Access Board guides):** door clear width 32 in minimum (36 in if the doorway is deeper than 24 in), measured stop to door face with the door open 90°, no projections below 34 in — [Access Board ch. 4](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-4-entrances-doors-and-gates/). Walking-surface clear width 36 in (915 mm) minimum, with an exception allowing 32 in for up to 24 in of length — [Corada copy of 2010 Standards §403.5](https://www.corada.com/documents/2010ADAStandards/403-5). Turning space: 60 in diameter circle, or a T-shape 60×60 in with 36 in arms; clear floor space 30×48 in — [Access Board ch. 3](https://www.access-board.gov/ada/guides/chapter-3-clear-floor-or-ground-space-and-turning-space)
- **NFPA 101 egress capacity factors (assembly):** 0.2 in/person for level components (doors, ramps) and 0.3 in/person for stairs, Table 7.3.3.1 (2021 ed.). NFPA 101 does not allow the IBC's reduction to 0.2 for stairs in sprinklered buildings with voice evacuation — [MeyerFire, exit capacity factor](https://www.meyerfire.com/university/what-is-an-exit-capacity-factor); [MeyerFire, IBC vs NFPA 101](https://meyerfire.com/university/difference-of-ibc-nfpa-101-exit-capacity-factors) (secondary)
- **Occupant load factors (Table 7.3.1.2):** concentrated assembly use without fixed seating 7 ft² net per person; less concentrated (tables and chairs) 15 ft² net — [Illinois State Fire Marshal](https://sfm.illinois.gov/content/dam/soi/en/web/sfm/sfmdocuments/documents/calculating-occupant-loads-for-assembly-occupancies-march-2022.pdf); [iDigHardware](https://idighardware.com/2014/07/decoded-calculating-the-occupant-load/). The 5 ft² standing-space figure is an IBC value — [iDigHardware Decoded Sep 2014](https://idighardware.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/Decoded-Sep14-Calculating-Occupant-Load.pdf). An exam-prep guide lists 3 ft² for NFPA 101 waiting spaces (unverified) — [OpenExamPrep](https://open-exam-prep.com/study-guides/nfpa-cfi-i/occupancy-classification-construction/occupant-load-calculations)
- **NFPA 101 assembly travel distance:** 200 ft unsprinklered and 250 ft sprinklered (§12.2.6); common path 20 ft (75 ft for under 50 occupants); dead ends 20 ft — [Decatur GA plan review checklist](https://www.decaturga.com/sites/default/files/fileattachments/fire/page/8146/plan_reveiw_assembly_spaces.pdf) (secondary)
- **Walking speed:** Pathfinder default 1.19 m/s; SFPE 43.4 m/min (≈0.72 m/s) in a dense-flow comparison; NFPA 130 37.7 m/min — [Pathfinder NFPA 130 comparison](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2026-1/pathfinder/examples/applications/comparing-nfpa-130/). Standard deviation of free walking speed is about 0.25 m/s (Lord 2005, via a Thunderhead presentation) — [Salyers 2011 presentation](https://files.thunderheadeng.com/femtc/2011_d2-14-salyers-presentation.pdf)
- **Effective width / boundary layer:** in SFPE-style hand calculations, flow = specific flow × effective width, where effective width = clear width minus a boundary layer — [KoreaScience paper](https://koreascience.kr/article/CFKO201030153581375.pdf). Pathfinder's door verification uses a 15 cm boundary layer per SFPE — [Pathfinder IMO_04](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2025-1/pathfinder/appendices/verification-validation/imo/imo-4)
- **Densities:** Pathfinder SFPE mode: max room density 3.55 p/m², optimal door-flow density 1.88 p/m² — [Pathfinder SFPE reference](https://www.thunderheadeng.com/docs/2026-1/pathfinder/appendices/technical-reference/sfpe). For context, Itaewon reached about 8–11 p/m² — [Korea Times](https://www.koreatimes.co.kr/www/nation/2023/09/113_343539.html)
- **Copyright:** *SBCCI v. Veeck* (5th Cir. en banc, 2002) held model codes enter the public domain "as law" once adopted but keep protection "as model codes". In *ICC v. UpCodes* (S.D.N.Y. 2020) summary judgment was denied; the court said liability "will not be for accurate copying of the I-Codes as adopted". *ASTM v. Public.Resource.Org* concerns standards incorporated by reference; the district court relied on *Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org* (2020) — [FMG Law](https://www.fmglaw.com/?p=11234); [ENR](https://www.enr.com/articles/48354-who-owns-the-building-code)

### Inferences
- **Fix for the solver:** either (a) apply 1.3 p/s/m to `effective_width = clear_width − 2×0.15 m` for doors, or (b) keep clear width but call the parameter "declared, applied to clear width (optimistic versus the SFPE effective-width method)". With an 0.85 m door, effective width is 0.55 m, so capacity per 2 s step drops from floor(1.3×0.85×2)=2 to floor(1.3×0.55×2)=1. That changes demo numbers, so decide before tuning the fixture.
- `walk_speed_m_s = 1.2` matches Pathfinder's 1.19 m/s free-walking default, and the docs above support it as a citation. Note that at evacuation densities, SFPE speeds are lower (about 0.7 m/s at the NFPA 130 comparison density).
- A useful extra, still "declared", output: floor area ÷ 15 ft² (tables) or ÷ 7 ft² (standing/chairs) ≈ a rough occupant-load reference. Compare required egress width (occupants × 0.2 in) with the measured sum of exit widths. Label it "NFPA 101 general factor, not the SF Fire Code occupant load, not an approved capacity". The plan currently lists "IBC occupant load" as cannot measure. Keep that, but the NFPA reference number could be shown as declared context. The team should decide whether this risks reading as a capacity approval. If in doubt, leave it out.
- Copyright wording: the plan's "code text not public domain" for the IBC is defensible for the model code but oversimplified. Adopted code text has contested status. Safer wording: "IBC/NFPA text is copyrighted model code; we cite section numbers and published factors, not code text." The 2010 ADA Standards are a federal publication (DOJ/Access Board), and their guides are freely posted by the Access Board.
